From Excel to Streamlit: Building Modern Supply Chain Tools with Python

After years of building and maintaining complex Excel-based MRP systems, I transitioned to creating an interactive web application using Python and Streamlit. This piece walks through the motivation, the build process, and the results.

What the Tool Does:

  • Accepts Excel/CSV uploads for demand history, BOMs, and inventory data
  • Applies machine learning models for demand forecasting
  • Runs full MRP calculations — net requirements, planned orders, exception messages
  • Generates interactive dashboards for KPI tracking

Why It Matters:

  • Excel MRP systems are error-prone and don't scale beyond a certain complexity
  • Python enables automation, version control, and reproducibility
  • Streamlit makes it accessible — no web development experience needed
  • The tool is freely available and can be adapted by any supply chain team

The Human Side of ERP Implementation: Lessons from Four Rollouts

Having led or co-led the implementation of four different ERP and WMS systems — SAGE X3, SYSPRO 7, MS Dynamics, and Cobi WMS — I've learned that technology is never the hardest part. Here are the lessons that matter.

Key Takeaways:

  • Start with why: If end-users don't understand the purpose, no amount of training will drive adoption
  • Clean data first: Migrating bad data into a new system just makes bad data faster
  • Champions, not mandates: Identify and empower influential team members to lead adoption from within
  • Plan for the dip: Productivity always drops after go-live. Budget for it in timelines and expectations
  • Celebrate wins visibly: Early successes — even small ones — build momentum and silence skeptics

2024 Humanitarian Logistics

What the Commercial Sector Can Learn from Humanitarian Supply Chains

My time at One Acre Fund — delivering farm inputs to 450,000+ smallholder farmers across rural Kenya — taught me principles that apply far beyond the humanitarian sector.

Transferable Lessons:

  • Resilience over efficiency: In environments where infrastructure is unreliable, building buffers and contingencies isn't waste — it's survival
  • The last mile defines the customer experience: A perfectly optimized warehouse means nothing if the final delivery fails
  • Standardize what you can, adapt what you must: Our SOPs reduced turnaround times by 20%, but field teams retained autonomy to solve local problems
  • Data in difficult places: Geotagging 2,500+ delivery sites gave us visibility that many commercial operations lack
